# Resources and readings

See the [reading plan](reading-plan.md) for every paper with its link, and what to read by when. To find a research idea and the papers behind it, see [finding ideas and papers](finding-ideas-and-papers.md).

## Causal inference and empirical research

- Scott Cunningham, [*Causal Inference: The Mixtape*](https://mixtape.scunning.com/): the main companion for the course's causal-inference session.
- Nick Huntington-Klein, [*The Effect*](https://theeffectbook.net/): accessible explanations of research design and causal inference.
- [Mastering 'Metrics resources](https://www.masteringmetrics.com/resources/): empirical examples and supporting material.

## Learning and coding with AI

- Eric J. Ma, [How AI turbocharges your learning](https://ericmjl.github.io/blog/2026/7/27/ai-turbocharges-learning/): recommended guide for using AI as a Socratic tutor and coding-learning partner rather than a shortcut.
- Read the course's [responsible LLM-use guide](../guides/llm-use.md) before using AI for coding, writing, or research.

## Writing and presenting

- Jesse Shapiro, [Four Steps to an Applied Micro Paper](https://shapiro.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum7731/files/2026-02/foursteps.pdf): a short note on how to write an empirical paper; read it before the writing session.
- Jesse Shapiro, [How to Give an Applied Micro Talk](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/shapiro/files/applied_micro_slides.pdf): for your first and final presentations.
- Robert Neugeboren with Mireille Jacobson, [Writing Economics](https://hwpi.harvard.edu/files/econ/files/writingec_f2014_0_1.pdf): a guide to writing an economics paper.
- [Great Economics Writing](https://github.com/Mixtape-Sessions/Great-Economics-Writing): a user-created catalog of well-written economics papers.
- [Finding ideas and papers](finding-ideas-and-papers.md): where ideas come from, where to search, which journals.

## Difference-in-differences and research design

- Baker, Callaway, Cunningham, Goodman-Bacon, and Sant'Anna (2026), [Difference-in-Differences Designs: A Practitioner's Guide](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20251650), *Journal of Economic Literature*.
- [Mixtape Sessions on GitHub](https://github.com/Mixtape-Sessions): slides and code for causal inference, difference-in-differences, instrumental variables, and regression discontinuity.

## AI and the labor market

- Strömberg, Lei, and Wu (2026), [The Generative AI Learning Penalty](https://ssrn.com/abstract=6868618).
- Autor et al. (2026), [Does AI Assistance Enhance or Erode Expertise?](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35720/w35720.pdf), NBER Working Paper 35720.
- Pissarides and Sehnbruch (2026), [AI and labour market inequalities: where to start](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/inequalities/2026/09/29/ai-and-labour-market-inequalities-where-to-start/), LSE blog.
- Imas and Schaal (2026), [Has AI impacted the labor market yet?](https://aleximas.substack.com/p/has-ai-impacted-the-labor-market)

## How LLMs work, and how to use them in research

- [How LLMs Work: A Visual Deep Dive](https://ynarwal.github.io/how-llms-work/): based on Andrej Karpathy's lecture.
- [Workshop: Using LLMs for data collection and annotation in the social sciences](https://github.com/sodascience/workshop_llm_data_collection) (Fang, Bernardo, and van Kesteren; CC BY 4.0).
- [Project APE: Autonomous Policy Evaluation](https://ape.socialcatalystlab.org) (Social Catalyst Lab, University of Zurich).

## Labor economics and policy

- [IZA World of Labor](https://wol.iza.org/): short evidence-based articles on labor-market policy and institutions.
- [MIT OpenCourseWare: Labor Economics I](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-661-labor-economics-i-fall-2024/resources/lecture-notes/): optional advanced notes on labor supply, demand, human capital, and unemployment.
- [European Labour Economics teaching materials](https://labourecoeu.ec.unipi.it/teaching-material/): optional material on European institutions and active labor-market policies.

## Data and replication

- [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/): accessible indicators and documented sources for motivation and descriptive context.
- [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat): European labor-market data.
- [OECD Data Explorer](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/): international labor-market and policy indicators.

## Required papers

The required paper PDFs are provided through Canvas, where copyright and access conditions can be respected. This repository links to openly available resources but does not redistribute publisher PDFs.
Start with the [data sources guide](data-sources.md), then use the [reading plan](reading-plan.md) to prepare the focal paper for each session.
