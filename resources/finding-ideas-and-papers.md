# Finding a research idea, and the papers behind it

## Where ideas come from

- **Political relevance:** what you and your friends find interesting; what is in the news or in a policy debate.
- **Academic relevance:** what is already established, and where you can add to the field.

## What makes a contribution

- A novel dataset with value.
- A novel idea or method. Often this is an established method applied to a new question: a new technology shock, new regulation that nobody has analyzed, or a topic that is now in the media and has not been studied like this before.

## Know the status quo

Search Google Scholar, ask your favorite AI, read abstracts, skim reports, until you find something. **Verify it:** does the paper exist, and is the question really open? Pick something you enjoy doing and think is important.

## From a fact to a paper

1. Start with a fact in the data: a gap, a trend, a change.
2. Ask why, and write the question in one sentence.
3. Search with three keywords. Read the abstract and introduction of three papers.
4. Follow the references, and "cited by" in Google Scholar.
5. Ask what is still open, and what data you would need.

## Where to look for motivation

- Reports: [OECD](https://www.oecd.org) Employment Outlook, [ILO](https://www.ilo.org) World Employment and Social Outlook, [WIFO](https://www.wifo.ac.at) and [IHS](https://www.ihs.ac.at), [AMS labor market data](https://www.ams.at/arbeitsmarktdaten).
- Press: The Economist, Financial Times, Der Standard, Die Presse.

## Where to find papers

- Working papers: [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers), [IZA discussion papers](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp), [CEPR](https://cepr.org/publications/discussion-papers), [SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com).
- Search: [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com), [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org).
- Overviews: chapters of the *Handbook of Labor Economics*; survey articles in the *Journal of Economic Literature*.

## Which journals

- General top journals: American Economic Review, Quarterly Journal of Economics, Journal of Political Economy, Econometrica, Review of Economic Studies.
- Labor economics: Journal of Labor Economics, Journal of Human Resources, Labour Economics, ILR Review.
- Applied work: American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, Journal of Public Economics.
- Rankings: [IDEAS/RePEc journal ranking](https://ideas.repec.org/top/top.journals.all.html), [SCImago](https://www.scimagojr.com).

A journal name is a signal, not proof: judge a paper by its design.
