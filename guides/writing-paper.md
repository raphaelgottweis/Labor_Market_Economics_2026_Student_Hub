# Writing an empirical economics paper

## Put the answer early

The reader should quickly learn what question you answer, what evidence you use, and what you find. Do not begin with a long literature review or a history of your project.

## Make every claim concrete

State the population, setting, treatment, outcome, comparison, and magnitude. Replace “the results are significant” with the result itself and its interpretation.

## Explain the design in plain language

Do not rely on a method label. Explain why the comparison group represents the missing counterfactual, what assumption makes it credible, and what could still go wrong.

## Use figures and tables as evidence

Give each figure a message title. Define variables, samples, units, and sources in a note. Discuss what the figure means, not merely that it exists.

## Cite accurately

Read and cite the original study when possible. Do not cite a paper you have not checked. Bibliographic software helps organise references but does not verify claims.

## Revise for clarity

One paragraph should make one point. Prefer short, direct sentences and active verbs. Delete background, equations, and robustness exercises that do not help answer the central question.
