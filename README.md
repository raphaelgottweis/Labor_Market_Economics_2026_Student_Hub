# Labor Market Economics 2026

Student information hub for `0909 Specialization Course – Labor Market Economics` at WU Vienna.

## What this course does

This is a hands-on course in empirical labor economics. We use economic models, data, and empirical papers to study current labor-market questions. You will work in a two-person team on an empirical research project; there is no final exam.

## What belongs here

- course information and deadlines;
- assignment instructions and templates;
- guides for reading papers, developing research projects, writing, and responsible LLM use;
- links to open resources and reading references.

## What does not belong here

Copyrighted paper PDFs, restricted data, private student work, grades, and editable instructor slides are not published in this repository. Required readings and protected course files are shared through Canvas.

## Assessment

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Idea pitch | 15% |
| First project presentation | 20% |
| Final presentation | 25% |
| Short paper | 35% |
| Participation and paper discussions | 5% |

Start with the [project roadmap](guides/research-project.md), the [data sources guide](resources/data-sources.md), then read the [assignment instructions](assignments/README.md).

## Course rhythm

Most four-hour meetings combine instructor input, discussion of an empirical paper, and project work. Bring the assigned paper and one substantive question to class.

## Course calendar

Sessions are 14:00 to 18:00 unless noted. Details and links that involve student data are on Canvas.

| Date | Topic | What you bring or do |
|---|---|---|
| 8 Oct (3h) | Introduction; labor supply and the household (Becker); finding a research idea | Find a teammate by topic. |
| 15 Oct | Labor supply: empirical evidence | Idea pitches, first half of the teams: slides due 1 day before. |
| 22 Oct | Women, war, and wages; child penalties; working with LLMs | Idea pitches, second half. Read the three labor demand papers by 5 Nov. 29 Oct: no class, work on your project and data. |
| 5 Nov | Labor demand; research designs; project lab | Bring your laptop and the data source from your pitch. Read Shapiro's *Four Steps to an Applied Micro Paper* (3 pages). |
| 12 Nov | No class | Work on your paper and data. Think about your question for Dr. Kopf and listen to the podcast "Geht so. Passt schon. Oder?". |
| 19 Nov | Discrimination and pay transparency | Your group's paper in full; abstract and introduction of the other six. Groups present, 15 + 5 minutes. |
| 26 Nov | First presentations, all teams | 5 minutes plus 5 minutes discussion. |
| 3 Dec | Unemployment and active labor market policy | Read Kasy and Lehner, and Cheung et al.: introduction and main results. |
| 10 Dec | AI and the labor market; prepare for Dr. Kopf (policy proposals and your questions) | Read the introduction and figures of *Canaries in the Coal Mine*. Questions for Dr. Kopf are due 14 Dec. |
| 17 Dec | Final presentations (10:00 to 14:00 and 14:00 to 19:00) | 20 minutes per team including feedback. Send slides 1 day before. |
| 14 Jan 2027, 14:00 to 16:00 | Guest discussion with Dr. Johannes Kopf (AMS), in German | Your questions, in the shared sheet by 14 Dec. |

With 34 students, the course has 17 two-person teams. Another team gives feedback on each pitch and presentation.
